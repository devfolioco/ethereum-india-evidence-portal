Ethereum for Institutional Finance in India

Indian finance has started putting assets on distributed ledgers. ETHIndia Institutions is where banks, market infrastructure, regulators and builders work out what role Ethereum and its ecosystem should play in that.

Why India

India is already tokenising.

  • The regulator mandated the ledger first.

    SEBI has required a distributed ledger for corporate-bond covenant monitoring since April 2022. The depositories, NSDL and CDSL, run it, and no corporate bond activates without it.

  • The first tokenised bonds are live.

    In September 2026 the RBI and SEBI launched a tokenised corporate-bond rail together. It raised ₹1,025 croreT1 across three issuers, settled in wholesale CBDC.

  • The markets are large and slow.

    India has ₹53.6 lakh croreT1 of corporate bonds outstanding and trades about ₹7,645 croreT1 of them a day. Away from the exchange a trade can take three to seven days to settle.

  • The flows are the world's largest.

    India receives $135 billionT1 a year in remittances, more than any other country.

Where Ethereum fits

The rail underneath is still open.

India's first tokenised bonds run on a closed ledger owned by its stock depositories. What comes next could run on that model, on a ledger shared between institutions, or on a public chain like Ethereum.

Ethereum gives institutions a settlement layer that no single one of them controls, the largest pool of smart-contract developers and tooling, and a network that has run since 2015. Its open problem for institutions is confidentiality, which has to be built in a layer above the chain. The research sets out where Ethereum fits in India and where it does not.
